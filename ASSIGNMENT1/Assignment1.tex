\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Variable
\emph{avganncount}}{Variable avganncount}}\label{variable-avganncount}

\emph{avganncount} is a numerical variable representing the ``Mean
number of reported cancer cases diagnosed annually (2010-2015).'' The
difference between the mean (623.2) and the median (175) indicates that
the distribution is not symmetric and deviates from normality.
Additionally, the first and third quartiles (80 and 509, respectively)
are much lower than the mean of 623.2 and the maximum value of 38150.0,
further suggesting skewness. The boxplot confirms this skewness, with an
extended upper whisker and numerous outliers, indicating a heavy right
tail. It has 273 outliers, and 224 of these are severe outliers.

The histogram overlaid with a normal curve highlights the deviation from
a normal distribution with the same mean and standard deviation. This
variable has no missing values. Lastly, the skeweness indicator and
Shapiro test rejection of the null hypothesis also confirm the
non-normal distribution of the variable.

:}{Variable target\_deathrate :}}\label{variable-target_deathrate}

Variable \emph{target\_deathrate} is a numerical variable that is the
target of the model. It contains no missing values in the train sample.
The median (178.3) and mean (178.8) values are really close, expressing
the symmetry of the distribution of the variable. Thanks to the boxplot
and the histogram we can graphically confirm the symmetry of the
distribution.

We performed the Shapiro test to check for the normality of the data,
and obtained a small p-value that rejects the null hypothesis,
indicating that the variable does not follow a normal distribution.
Therefore, the target variable is not normally distributed.

Moreover, it contains 35 outliers, 1 of which is a severe.
